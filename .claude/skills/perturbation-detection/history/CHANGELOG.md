# Changelog — perturbation-detection task pack

Lifted out of `prompt.md` on 2026-09-21. It was never sent to the model and
never read by the code; it is the record of how the criteria got here.
The criteria themselves are `criteria/prompt.md`.

## Changelog

- **0.0.26: a rename; no rule changes.** Stage B's "cap" is now called the **damaged text downgrade**, everywhere outside `history/`: in the prompt the model reads, in the code, in the docs and in the tests. The record field `validation.stage_b_capped` is now `validation.damaged_text_downgrade`, the summary CSV column follows it, the `decide.yaml` table `cap:` is `damaged_text_downgrade:`, `reason_rules.cap_reason` is `downgrade_reason`, and the progress flag `STAGE-B-CAP` is `DAMAGED-TEXT-DOWNGRADE`. Every record written before this carries the old field name, so the readers go through `task.rules.downgraded()`, which accepts either; without it a comparison against an old baseline would read every old downgrade as released. Documents under `history/` keep the old word, because they are the record of what was said at the time.
- **0.0.25: Stage B's trigger stops being an adjective. `text_defects` is required; no criterion below Step 0 changes.** The cap fired on `processing_status = "partial" OR text_completeness != "full"` — two paper-level self-reports of the text's quality with no evidence behind either — and that trigger was **not reproducible: 3 of 30 papers changed it between two byte-identical runs at v0.0.23, and 4 of 24 at v0.0.24 after the `ASSEMBLY:` block had removed the largest single cause.** Reading those eight flips decided the design. **Three quarters of them were adjudicable by a quote the model had already read**, and the fourth named no locus at all: `s41586-023-06981-x` was caught by the run that quoted its break verbatim — *"Between 18 and 22 GW, tissue growth had pressed these ventricular walls close together (Fig. 3b,c and"* — while the other run reported `full`; `2021.09.16.460628` cited dangling cross-references to methods that are absent (*"see Computational Methods"*, *"see Methods"*), corroborated by the extractor finding no methods-labelled text at all; `atvbaha.122.317953` claimed `truncated` and pointed at nothing. So the trigger is now **the harness having withheld text, or a defect whose quote verifies against the source it cites**. Both paper-level fields stay on the record, stay the model's own summary, and stop deciding anything. **Per-source, because the instability partly WAS the schema's fault.** `science.aat1699` flipped between two true statements — one run described the garbled supplement, the other the intact main article — and nothing could hold both. `text_defects` entries carry a `source_id`, and `processing_status = "partial"` with `text_completeness = "full"` is therefore **legal and meaningful**: one source is garbage, the article is whole. An earlier draft of this change proposed collapsing those two fields as contradictory; the runs refuted it, verbatim (*"supp2 … arrived as a large run of mojibake … the main article, including a complete Methods section, supp1 and supp3 are complete"*). **Scope, which is a curator decision of 2026-09-16:** a defect in the **main** source caps, and `no_methods_content` caps from **any** source, because the cap withholds a negative only where the missing text could have carried the pairing sentence — a garbled table in a reporting summary could not, and an absent methods section could wherever it lives. Under the old rule `10.1126/science.aat1699` sat at `unclear` over three garbled supplementary tables while curator ruling 6 on that paper is `no`. **`no_methods_content` is the one kind with no quote**, because an absence has no text to copy; the harness FALSIFIES it instead, against the per-section character counts `harness.prepare` now records — and against a measured threshold rather than the label's presence, because `aat1699` carries 228 characters of `methods`-labelled text that are two copies of the heading, while the median corpus paper carries 28,467. A check keyed on the label existing would have refuted a true claim and released the cap on the one text in this corpus that is genuinely broken. **Two asymmetries to keep straight.** A quote the harness checks and cannot find is a claim REFUTED: dropped, no cap, the negative stands. An entry it cannot read at all is a claim UNKNOWN, and **caps** — the v0.0.5 lesson that a safety mechanism a typo switches off is not one, which the old trigger got backwards by testing enum membership before the comparison. **Guards, from the v0.0.10 episode:** precedence stated first (decide the text's integrity, then record why — the array never creates a judgment), `[]` named as the normal answer with the measured count beside it, the pipeline's own cuts named concretely as non-defects, and the same verifier and threshold as `perturbations[]`. **And the v0.0.24 contradiction is closed:** Step 0 defined `"full"` as nothing missing beyond what `ASSEMBLY:` reports, which made `"full"` the correct answer on a budget-truncated paper while `harness.validate` overrode it — both rung-3 papers did exactly that, twice. A budget truncation `ASSEMBLY:` reports is now a defect to report, so the model reaches the harness's answer instead of being corrected into it.

- **0.0.24: the text pipeline now says what it removed, and "full" is defined against that. No criterion is touched.** Step 0 asked whether the text was complete while nothing in the model-facing prompt said which cuts were the pipeline's own. Every source arrives with its reference list, acknowledgments, funding, competing-interest and data-availability sections and all back matter removed; no table and no figure image is supplied at all; and on a long paper Discussion and Introduction are dropped to fit the budget. **The consequence is measured, not theoretical: 154 of the 392 corpus papers end on a bare heading with nothing under it — "Associated Data", "Supplementary Materials", "Footnotes" — because the exclusion list took the content and left the label, and 192 end without terminal punctuation.** `10.1182/bloodadvances.2023011445` is the case that forced this: in the two v0.0.23 acceptance runs it reported `text_completeness` = "full" and then "truncated" on byte-identical input (prompt sha256 equal, `validation.stage_a` = "no" both times, same single IL-6 perturbation at `single_cell_paired` = "no"), Stage B capped one run and not the other, and the determination moved `no` → `unclear`. Every harness fact about that paper says complete — JATS origin, 0.98 section coverage, all five body sections found, its one supplement fetched and read, truncation ladder at rung 0 — and the text it was handed ends on exactly such a dangling heading. **3 of 30 papers, ~10%, flipped this self-report between those two runs while Stage A flipped on 0 of 30.** So the fix is not to move the judgment: `text_completeness` stays the model's call, on the text it was actually given, and a new `ASSEMBLY:` block states the facts that call needs — which sources were supplied and how big, how many supplementary files of how many found, which sections were removed, which content kinds reached the model, and which budget rung ran. `ASSEMBLY:` is rendered per paper by `harness.prepare.assembly_note` from the assembly that just happened, never written into this file, so it cannot go stale and is byte-identical between two runs of the same paper. **"full" now means: nothing is missing beyond what `ASSEMBLY:` says was removed**, and the pipeline's own cuts are named as non-defects so they cannot be read as truncation. **What was considered and rejected, with the measurement, in `DESIGN-stage-b-gate.md`:** re-keying Stage B's cap onto a harness-measured fact instead of the self-report. The corpus's extraction sidecars carry a `section_labelling` block (coverage, `body_sections_found` / `body_sections_missing`, confidence) and a declared/fetched/read supplement ledger, and against the 15 capped papers every predicate they support is unusable — `coverage < 0.50` fires on 11 papers and covers 0 of the 15, `confidence ∈ (none, low)` gives precision 0.14, and the union of all of them fires on 146 of 392 while still missing 4. Those fields measure whether the extractor could LABEL the structure, not whether content is absent: `science.abc3172` has 0.99 coverage with no methods label anywhere, and `science.abo7257` has 0.00 coverage and is correctly "full". Keying the cap on `truncation.rung > 0` instead would fire on 2 papers of 392 and delete the mechanism. **No schema change, no new required field** — the v0.0.10 attractor episode is why an evidence sub-object was NOT added in the same version as the change that may make it unnecessary.

- **0.0.23: the curator batch of 2026-09-15, and the contradiction it found.** Twelve rulings from three curators, scored against the stored v0.0.22 corpus without a re-run: **6 matched, 4 did not, and 2 could not be expressed at all.** Two of the six matches re-confirm rulings 7 and 8 from readers who had not seen them. **The batch's most important finding is a contradiction that predates it.** The EXPOSURE bullet said "a pathogen: infection is exposure" while the exclusion table said `observational_disease_state` covers "a naturally occurring disease state... with no bench manipulation" — and a naturally acquired human infection satisfies both, with nothing to break the tie. The corpus resolved it BOTH WAYS on the same shape: 34 papers suppressed a natural infection, 21 of them landing `no` with `would_have_paired: "yes"`, while 13 comparable papers landed `yes`. The tie is now broken by **who applied the exposure**. An exposure the investigators applied keeps ruling 9's answer — a perturbation whether or not the paper is named after it, subject only to carrying a contrast. An exposure that occurred OUTSIDE the study — a natural infection, a medication the patient was already taking, an environmental history — is a perturbation only where the paper's stated question is the response to it. That is what separates a COVID paper asking what SARS-CoV-2 did to nasal epithelium (report it) from a lung atlas that happens to include COVID-19 donors beside IPF and healthy ones (do not). **This was load-bearing for the second change, not merely adjacent to it.** `10.1016/j.cell.2021.07.023` is a curator-confirmed `yes`, and under v0.0.22 it was `yes` on *corticosteroids* while the infection the curator actually cited sat in `suppressed_candidates`. Right answer, false premise — the ruling-1 shape. Suppressing the corticosteroids alone, which the clinical-therapy sharpening below does, would have flipped a confirmed `yes` to `no`; the two changes only work together. **Clinical therapy, and the prominence of a mention.** `10.1038/s41467-024-55440-2` grouped 3 of 10 sequenced donors by progestin contraception and was called `yes` on the ground that the arm "is used as a grouping variable" — which is precisely what sharpening 1 already forbids. So sharpening 1 gains the missing half: WEIGH the mention. One body mention, a donor-table footnote and one supplementary panel, against headline axes of menopause and cycle phase, is a cohort attribute the authors recorded rather than a question they asked, and an incidental attribution in one clause does not promote it. What makes something the subject is the paper saying so where papers say such things — title, abstract, stated gap, conclusions. `incidental_clinical_therapy` now points at that sharpening explicitly, because it is the rule that attracts this exact error. **`not_applicable`, and the question Step 0 was not asking.** A review has no place in a yes/no/unclear vocabulary: `10.1016/j.coi.2022.102188` yielded six perturbations and twelve sample groups harvested from work its authors did not do, and came out `unclear`. Reporting `no` would assert the opposite of what the text shows; `unclear` claims a deficiency in text that is complete. Step 0b asks whether the paper reports a study of its own and, when it does not, returns immediately the way a failed extraction does — `not_applicable`, empty arrays, article type in `ambiguities`. **The value is NOT added to `labels`**, which is also the enum for `has_single_cell_assay` and `perturbation_present_any_assay`; a new `determination_labels` set covers `perturbation_present` alone, because widening the shared set would have made `has_single_cell_assay: "not_applicable"` legal. Stage A gains **A-1**, evaluated after A0 (a paywall page is `unclear`, not "a review") and deliberately not folded into A1 (a gated paper's empty array means Step 0b returned, not that nothing was found). **Guards, taken wholesale from the v0.0.10 episode where adding `suppressed_candidates` moved 2 of 6 determinations with no criterion edited:** the gate is stated to fire rarely; its negatives are named concretely rather than generically, and the first of them is that **a re-analysis or integration atlas is still primary research**; article type is decided before any experiment is looked at, so "these experiments look like someone else's" cannot reach it; and CC-8 catches the contradiction from either side. **`no` beats `unclear` once you can name the readout.** `10.1016/j.immuni.2020.03.019` was scored `unclear` while its own stated grounds were that "every reported readout of it is flow cytometry and the scRNaseq populations were sorted separately" — a verbatim restatement of the `"no"` bullet. `unclear` is now explicitly for when the readout assay cannot be identified, not for residual unease about a call already made. Corpus-wide this reaches at most 4 papers, the whole `pairing_not_stated` population, and Stage B's 14 degraded-text `unclear`s are untouched. **Two curator answers are deliberately NOT adopted, both recorded in `../criteria/rulings.md` with the reasoning.** Curator 1's `no` on `10.1038/s41467-021-25125-1`: the paper's own quote-verified text says the formalin and rotarod nuclei are "11.0% of the total neuronal cells/nuclei" and that it compared "nuclei from an experimental condition and all naïve cells/nuclei", so perturbed samples really are in the analysed set — reused public data still counts, and the question of whether the incorporated samples were the treated ones goes back to the curator. Curator 2's `yes?` on `10.1016/j.healun.2026.02.1666`: a resolved rejection episode is a state nobody applied, per rulings 2, 7 and 8 and curator 3's reading of the same boundary. **Determination movement is PREDICTED, not measured:** ~8 of 21 infection papers `no`→`yes`, one contraception paper `yes`→`no`, one review `unclear`→`not_applicable`, two `unclear`→`no`. The acceptance protocol measures it before any full corpus run, because predicted and measured are different claims.

- **0.0.17: one governing question, stated once, and the third differently-keyed rule is retired.** Three rules in Step 2 were asking the same question in three keys. The clinical-therapy rule asked *"attributed to the treatment, or the study's setting?"* — correct. v0.0.16's disease-model rule asked *"was the purpose to induce the disease?"* — refuted by rulings 9 and 10 the day it shipped. The derivation rule asked *"identity or factor?"* — refuted by the curator's clarification of 2026-09-06: **"IF the goal of the research is to study the differentiation cocktail - IT WILL BE a PERTURBATION, if it just means to get to some cell state/fate - it is NOT."** Both refuted keys were STRUCTURAL — they decided on the shape of the comparison rather than on what the paper concludes — and that is the same error three times over. So the question is now stated **once**, before the rules that use it: *is the applied thing what the paper is trying to LEARN ABOUT, or is it how the paper OBTAINED the material it then studies?* The exclusion reasons become labels for that one answer, and where a rule states its own test the test is explicitly an illustration the question overrides. `tests/test_spec_self_consistency.py` asserts the question appears exactly once in the model-facing text, because restating it while editing a rule is the cheap natural thing to do and is precisely what cost v0.0.7 two runs of one paper. **Three sharpenings ride with it, each of which cost a curator ruling and each of which is a case where the intuitive reading is wrong.** (1) *Being the axis you GROUP the data along is not being the SUBJECT* — `10.1038/s41467-021-21783-3` takes tissue across gestation and concludes that *"unlike alveolar differentiation during gestation"* the `Brca1/p53` effect is cell-autonomous, so gestation is the yardstick and `Brca1` loss is the subject; v0.0.16 read the grouping variable as the subject and returned `yes` against ruling 11. (2) *The shape of the comparison decides nothing* — "diseased against healthy" fits a western-diet NASH paper and an engrafted-tumour paper alike, and "a new identity against its precursor" fits a paper studying a cocktail and one merely using it alike. (3) *A manipulation applied on top of an already-established model, whose effect the paper reports, is a perturbation even though the model beneath it is suppressed* — `Trem2−/−` on `5XFAD` is a perturbation while `5XFAD` is the model, which was the last paper still unstable under v0.0.16 and was unstable because the cheap germline check swallowed it. **Pregnancy takes the escape hatch rather than a tenth `rule` value** (curator decision, 2026-09-06): a physiological state the investigators set up is recorded under `disease_model_establishment` with `why` saying the state is physiological rather than diseased. One paper is thin evidence for a new bucket, the prompt already directs an unlisted case to the closest value with an explanation, and the choice is reversible — where a tenth value would not have been. **Exposure, measured before the run rather than discovered after it:** 65 of 392 papers carry a `derivation_formulation` suppression, 25 of them are already `yes` so their determination cannot move, and **40 are not `yes` and could flip** if their formulation turns out to be the paper's subject. None of those 40 is in the 52-paper v0.0.16 acceptance set, so this version needs its own — `papers-accept-v0017.txt`, all 40.

- **0.0.16: the disease-model rule is re-keyed on ATTRIBUTION, because v0.0.15's tell was refuted by the first two papers the curator read.** v0.0.15 keyed the rule on the manipulation's PURPOSE and offered a structural tell — *"the sequenced contrast is diseased tissue against healthy — a STATE contrast"*. Curator rulings 9 and 10 (2026-09-03) are **`yes`** on two papers with exactly that structure: a western diet fed for 24-36 weeks whose Methods say *"To induce NAFLD and NASH"* (`10.1016/j.cell.2021.12.018`), and a T9 spinal contusion in an injury atlas (`10.1038/s41467-022-33184-1`), of which the curator said *"the researchers have delivered trauma to the spinal cord and then were studying the response to the trauma (perturbation)"*. So neither stated purpose nor contrast shape decides. **The key is attribution: does the paper characterise what the manipulation DID, or the MATERIAL it produced?** Attributed to it = perturbation; used to obtain material characterised for something else = model. **That is not a new test — it is the clinical-therapy governing question of v0.0.11, which already reads "Attributed to the treatment = VARIABLE = report it… Used to reveal a difference that was already there = SETTING = suppress it."** v0.0.15 wrote a second, differently-keyed rule where it should have widened the existing one, which is precisely how this repo generated its v0.0.7 and v0.0.12 contradictions; the two are now one question asked of a therapy or of a bench manipulation. **Rulings 12 and 13 supply a cheap first test, and it is stated first for that reason.** On `10.3389/fimmu.2023.1211505` (`Col7a1−/−` skin) and `10.1016/j.isci.2022.104097` (BTBR `ob/ob` and `UMOD-C125R` kidneys) the curator ruled `no`: *"we are studying the way sick cells are behaving"*, and *"not CAUSING the injury — instead studying the transcriptional signatures of tissues that appear to exhibit injury signs."* In both, **nothing was applied during the study at all** — the animals were bred or purchased in the diseased state. That is mechanically checkable, settles the whole germline class, and leaves the judgment-dependent attribution test only for cases where something genuinely was applied. Putting the cheap test first matters because an intent test is this pipeline's documented instability: v0.0.15 left three papers flipping across two runs of identical input, and all three were cases these two tests now answer. **A wild-type comparator arm decides nothing** — the diet, the contusion, the engraftment and the germline models all have one, and two of those four are perturbations. **Expected direction of movement:** v0.0.15 moved 11 papers off `yes`; re-keying should return the ones whose papers study the response to something applied — nerve constriction in a pain atlas, APAP in a liver-regeneration study, topical MC903 against vehicle, ischaemia-reperfusion in a kidney study, LAD ligation in cardiac-response papers — and keep the engraftment, the induced-allele and the germline models. It should also newly reach `science.aay3224`, whose Rag1 knockout is a germline genotype and was `yes` in both v0.0.15 runs; that is a prediction and not yet a measurement.

- **0.0.15: establishing a disease model is not perturbing it, and the closed `rule` set grows for the first time.** Two curator rulings of 2026-09-03 (`10.1016/j.ccell.2025.12.003`, `10.1016/j.cell.2021.11.031`) say that a manipulation whose purpose is to produce the disease state under study is the MODEL, not a perturbation of it — and the second ruling is why the rule is keyed on the STATE rather than on the route. One paper engrafted CRISPR-engineered AKPS tumour organoids; the other injected 4-hydroxytamoxifen through the colonoscope to activate Cre in resident cells against a PBS control, introducing nothing. A rule saying "cells were introduced" would have caught the first and missed the second. Nor does comparing two ways of making the model promote it: two cells of origin, two genotypes, or two induction protocols, all sequenced, is a contrast between models — exactly the move v0.0.11 made for two differentiation media, and this is its disease-state analogue. **What this took away from `perturbations`, and what it did not.** A manipulation applied to the ESTABLISHED model at fixed disease state is still a perturbation and is reported normally: a drug given to tumour-bearing mice against vehicle, a depleting antibody against isotype, a cytokine against untreated. The rule reaches only the step that made the diseased sample exist. **This version FIXES A CONTRADICTION IT INHERITED, rather than creating one.** v0.0.14's tricky-cases list said "Cell/animal model where the engineering is the studied point (e.g., an oncogene-transformed line, a transgenic disease model) = perturbation", and under that rule the extraction was right on both papers and said so. A curator ruling supersedes the prompt, and where they disagree that is a bug in the prompt — so the rule is rewritten, the Step 2 genetic bullet now defers to it (a knockout that produces the tumour is the model; a knockout compared against wild type to ask what the gene does is a perturbation), and Step 3's canonical pairing example no longer uses "tumors from Brca1-deleted mice underwent snRNA-seq", which had become an illustration of the wrong thing. **`disease_model_establishment` is the ninth `rule` value.** The set had been closed at eight since v0.0.10, and the reason the model reported a perturbation on both papers is that it had nowhere else to put the case: `observational_disease_state` is defined as a "naturally occurring disease state... with NO experimental manipulation", so an engineered or induced model is neither, and the prompt tells the model not to invent a value. That was a taxonomy gap, not a misreading. The new value is in `reasons_under_review`, so its papers surface at triage tier 2 for ratification rather than being discovered later; its entries will nearly always carry `would_have_paired: "yes"`, because an engraftment pairs trivially to the assay run on the tissue it produced. **No count of the `rule` set is written anywhere now.** "Eight" was stated in four places — three of them would have gone stale here, which is the v0.0.12 failure shape in miniature, and one of them was the cut-marker inside the drift test written to catch stale counts. `prompt.md`'s exclusion table owns the scopes, `record.yaml` owns the values, and neither says how many. **Expected determination movement:** measured before the change, 7 of the 115 `yes` papers have every paired perturbation reading as model establishment, and 16 rest entirely on genetic perturbations. The rule is written to reach the first group and to spare a genuine gene-function experiment, but which papers actually move is a measurement this version has not made yet.

- **0.0.14: the spec stops answering one question twice.** No new field, no new value set, no change to Stage A, Stage B or the truth table. What changed is that **fourteen places where this file said two different things about the same case now say one thing** — and one of those fourteen moves determinations, so it is stated first. **The clinical-therapy boundary is re-decided.** v0.0.11 added condition (iii) — *does the paper treat the therapy as its VARIABLE or its SETTING?* — because (i) and (ii) alone returned the wrong answer on `10.1038/s41467-025-65049-8`. But every clinical-therapy precedent in the file predated (iii) and none had been re-decided under it: **worked example 5** resolved a neoadjuvant-chemotherapy case to `single_cell_paired = yes` on (i) and (ii), and its own stated axis (tumour versus adjacent normal) is one of the signals the spec elsewhere lists as evidence the therapy is the SETTING — so the file's one worked positive for clinical therapy contradicted the rule that governs it. Worse, the `suppressed_candidates` precedence note declared that "report rules win" and gave "a named therapy tied to specific sequenced samples" as one of them — conditions (i) and (ii) standing alone, i.e. the exact test v0.0.11 replaced, asserted as *overriding* the rule that replaced it. A setting-type therapy tied to sequenced samples therefore had an explicit precedence claim on one side and an explicit suppression order on the other, with nothing to arbitrate. Example 5 is now decided under (iii) (it suppresses, and carries the flip case that would make it report), and the precedence note names all three conditions. `incidental_clinical_therapy` is the second-largest suppression reason at **86 of 392 papers**, so **determinations are expected to move here** — that is the point, not a side effect, and the direction is `yes` -> `no` on papers whose therapy is a setting. **The incidental rule's threshold is stated once.** The rule says any one of its three signals is enough and that none of them settles a named, tied therapy at all; the precedence note said "the three signals are met", conjunctive. They part company on every cohort that trips some but not all three — the majority case, and the one example 5 is built on. **The NOT list's blanket escape clause is gone.** The heading qualified the whole list with "unless the paper makes the item the manipulated variable", which is the defect v0.0.5 shipped and v0.0.6 named — in a pipeline-benchmarking paper the protocol *is* the manipulated variable, so the clause promoted exactly the cases the list exists to exclude. It was fixed for `sample_handling_protocol` only, and that rule announced itself as "the one place" the qualifier does not reach while three others were equally flat and said nothing. The heading now draws the line the rules actually use — an item is promoted when the paper applies it as a **biological** variable, never when it is the paper's **technical** variable — and **an eight-row table owns the exclusion reasons' scopes**, stating per rule whether promotion can reach it. That table also removes the overlap where `routine_processing` and `sample_handling_protocol` both claimed a media-brand comparison, a storage-duration series and a dissociation-enzyme benchmark: both fired on the corpus (63 papers against 27), so the tally the eight-value scheme exists to produce was splitting one population in two. Bucket labels move; determinations cannot, since a suppressed candidate never reaches Stage A. **Step 1 no longer tells the model to stop.** "Do not proceed to perturbation matching" on a no-assay paper contradicted two required fields defined over Step 2 regardless of assay, so such a paper had to either disobey the instruction or omit a required field. Dormant here — 0 of 392 papers have `has_single_cell_assay` other than "yes", the corpus being pre-filtered — and not dormant for the next pack. **A failed extraction's 0.0 is named as a sentinel** rather than left contradicting a rubric that would score it near 1.0, and **the partial-text confidence ceiling now applies only to negatives and unclears**, matching Stage B's deliberate asymmetry: missing text can hide a pairing but cannot invent one. Measured cost of the old wording — all three positives on partial text sat at confidence **exactly 0.38**, the ceiling rather than the evidence, which routed every one of them into triage tier 3 by rubric rather than by any judgment about the paper. Also: the model-facing text no longer names a `schema`/`prompt` version pair that 0.0.13 abolished; Step 2 no longer claims "(same criteria as before)" after five revisions of those criteria; the cell-line organism bullet no longer tells the model to infer a species the bullet above it forbids inferring; and the batch spec now describes the record the harness actually writes — it specified a `{"run": ..., "result": ...}` envelope with `run_id`, `error_code`, `input_tokens` and `assembled_text_sha256`, none of which appears in any record or anywhere in the code, while claiming a model-free "failed" record is written for an unfetched paper, which is the determination-shaped row for an unassessed paper that the rule it cites exists to forbid. **What holds this.** `tests/test_spec_self_consistency.py` (12 guards, one per finding, every parser failing loudly when it matches nothing) plus two in `test_prompt_pack_agree.py` binding the new table to the closed `rule` set — because owning the scopes means restating the eight names, and a value stated in N places and changed in fewer is what cost this project two runs of one paper at v0.0.7 and a 386/6 record split at v0.0.12. **Tests 165 -> 183.** The 392 stored records re-validate to a byte-identical summary CSV and a byte-identical 5,836-line review screen, at 140 issues and 2,471 of 2,471 quotes verified — because nothing outside the model's own reading changed. **Not yet done, and it is the part that matters: no paper has been re-scored under 0.0.14.** The determination effect of the clinical-therapy fix is predicted, not measured.

- **0.0.13: one version number, and it is no longer written in this file.** Replaces the `prompt_version` + `schema_version` pair with a single `task_version`, declared once in `task/task.yaml` and spliced into this file by `harness.prepare` as `{{TASK_VERSION}}` — the same mechanism as `{{PAPER_ID}}`. **No criterion changed and no determination logic changed:** Stage A, Stage B, the truth table, every Step 2 rule and the closed `rule` set are untouched, so `harness.validate`'s mirror and `tests/test_determination_v005.py` remain valid unchanged. The two numbers were separate only because somebody had to judge, per revision, whether the record shape had moved — and the cost of that judgment is measured. At v0.0.12 the answer was yes and three of the four declaration sites were updated; the model split on the contradiction, **386 of 392 corpus records followed the schema example and emitted 0.0.7 while 6 followed the instruction line and emitted 0.0.6**, and the validator was calibrated to the minority, filing a spurious issue on 386 correct records. The judgment is now replaced by two facts: `task_version`, which the harness grades a record against, and `pack_sha256`, a hash over every rule-bearing file that answers "were these two records produced under the same rules" by comparison rather than by opinion. A version bump says the author thought something changed; the hash says whether anything did. Records written before 0.0.13 carry `schema_version` and no `task_version`; `harness.validate` reads them as their run's `prompt_version` and says so, so the 392 already scored stay comparable without a re-run.

- **v0.0.12: the record says WHOSE sample was perturbed. `schema_version` 0.0.6 -> 0.0.7, two new fields, and NO determination-logic change.** Stage A, Stage B and the truth table are untouched, so `harness.validate`'s mirror and `tests/test_determination_v005.py` remain valid unchanged. - **v0.0.11: Two boundary rules whose written test pointed away from the curator's line, plus the precedence gap between them.** Curator rulings of 2026-08-28, taken after the 30-paper v0.0.10 evaluation (`EVAL-30-v0.0.10.md`). **No schema change and no determination-logic change** — Stage A, Stage B and the truth table are untouched, `schema_version` stays `0.0.6`, and `harness.validate`'s mirror plus `tests/test_determination_v005.py` remain valid unchanged.   1. **Directed differentiation is a target cell type, not a perturbation — the uniformity precondition is dropped.** v0.0.9 rule 4 excluded a derivation cocktail only when "given uniformly to every sample". Right answer, false premise. The curator's ruling is that the answer is right for a different reason: "different target cell type, not perturbation... I don't really care what has been applied to get to the final diff cell type." The test is now **what the manipulation is FOR** — a different target cell IDENTITY is the model; a named factor varied while identity is held fixed is a perturbation. The v0.0.6 carve-out is untouched, because it was always about a contrast in a *factor*, never in cell identity.
  2. **An applied clinical therapy is a perturbation only when the paper treats it as the VARIABLE, not the SETTING.** v0.0.10's guard 5 tested structure — "where two sequenced timepoints straddle the therapy... report it as a chemical perturbation, do not suppress it" — which cannot separate the two cases it now has to. `10.7554/elife.104978.2` says relapse involves a "chemotherapy-driven lineage switch": the drug changed the cells, so it stays a perturbation and that paper stays `yes`. `10.1038/s41467-025-65049-8` reports a ZBTB16+ blast population already present at diagnosis (0.67% at day 0, 97.6% at day 28) and delivers a **day-0** biomarker for refractoriness across a responder/non-responder cohort of 58 children: the therapy is the sieve, not the subject, and the curator ruled it not a perturbation. Both papers have two sequenced timepoints straddling chemotherapy, so only an attribution test separates them.
  3. **The precedence gap those two left.** Guard 5 ordered "report it"; the incidental-therapy rule offered three signals "any one of which is enough" to suppress. `s41467-025-65049-8` satisfied both and nothing said which won, so the model was arbitrating an unstated contradiction — the exact shape v0.0.7 fixed for temperature and the reason two runs of one paper can disagree. Guard 5 now settles only whether a condition was *unintended* and hands the perturbation question to the clinical-therapy rules; the incidental rule defers to the governing question. All four places that state these rules — the unintended-condition bullet, the derivation bullet, the report rule, and the toggle table — are changed together, since leaving one behind is what caused the v0.0.7 precedence bug.
